THE AI OBJECTIVE: CAPABILITY IS NOT FIDELITY

The model will catch things the regex missed (publickly), because it reads context. It will also "improve" words that were never errors (betwixt, hath, we was), because it was trained to produce fluent modern prose. The point of today is not whether Gemini is good; it is that the same diff against Tuesday's file measures both behaviours in numbers. Every AI tag is a proposal until a human has checked it against the manual file.

INVIOLABLE PEDAGOGICAL BOUNDARIES

1. Same 20 raw lines, fresh copy: the AI file starts from the same excerpt of transcription_reconciled.xml as Wednesday's regex file. Never feed the model the manual or regex file; it will copy the answers and the comparison is void.
2. AI output never goes straight into the edition: it is applied only to transcription_reconciled_ai.xml. Nothing reaches transcription_normalized.xml until it has been classified against the manual file.
3. A dropped <lb/> is a rejection, not a fix-up: if any line break or its n value is missing or moved, discard that output and re-prompt with "DO NOT DELETE OR MOVE ANY <lb/> TAGS." Do not repair the AI's file by hand and then count it as the AI's work.
4. Disclosure is part of the assignment: every resp="#gemini" must resolve to a <respStmt> in the header, and the <change> entry says what was audited and rejected. Students put their own name and id in the header, not the lesson's example name.

Reference for the Live Build

Master Prompt: The Five Parts

Persona     expert TEI P5 textual scholar
Task        conservative normalization + expansion
Authority   modern standard spelling (or Academie)
Negative    no synonyms, no grammar, no tag moves
Format      TEI fragment only, resp="#gemini"

Few-Shot Pair (Thorn and Abbreviation)

In:  yt was agreed by ye Govr at Halifax.
Out: <choice><abbr>yt</abbr><expan
     resp="#gemini">that</expan></choice> was
     agreed by ... <abbr>Govr</abbr><expan
     resp="#gemini">Governor</expan> ...

The Three Metrics for the AI Pass

HR        = hypercorrections / AI choice blocks
Precision = TP / (TP + FP)
Recall    = TP / (TP + FN)
Typical:  regex P 100% R 71%  |  AI P 75% R 86%

Cohort Specific AI Guidance

JAY · Emily Dickinson Poem (MS Am 1118.3)

  • The firewall is the prompt: no capital, dash or rhyme word may be touched; only opake and etherial are allowed targets. A model that adds a single comma has broken an explicit constraint, which is the strongest evidence Jay can report.
  • Expect Category C, not A: regex had nothing to say about the dashes and the AI may convert them. An HR above 50% on a tiny edit count is a legitimate result; say so in the reflection.

ACE · French Revolutionary Decree (1791)

  • Where the AI should win: conditional and plural forms (-oient) that Wednesday's whitelist did not list are likely Category A. Check each against the manual file before counting it.
  • Where it should lose: droit, L.S. and the decree's legal formulas must come back untouched; supplied accents on capitals (DÉCRET) belong in <reg> only if Tuesday's policy declared them.

OLYVIA · Maud Lewis Letter (20th c. Cursive)

  • Fix one line of the lesson's prompt: section 11 lists wont → won't as a target. Wednesday showed wont can be a word; have Olyvia write "only where it means will not" and check each hit.
  • Dialect erasure is the test: we was and double negatives must survive, and no <sic> may appear. Any change there is Category B, morphological or dialect erasure.

ANDROMEDA · 1752 Peace and Friendship Treaty

  • The pronoun ye: the prompt says expand ye to the, but in "Know ye that" it is the pronoun. If Gemini expands it, that is a hallucinated expansion; if it leaves it, that is context reading regex could not do. Either way it belongs in the reflection.
  • Titles vs common nouns: His Majesty, Governor and King keep capitals; Treaty and Articles may be lowercased in <reg>. Expansions of Capt. or Col. must stop at the word.
