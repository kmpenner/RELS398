THE NORMALIZATION OBJECTIVE: TWO LAYERS, NOTHING LOST

Normalization adds a readable layer without destroying the diplomatic one. Every change sits inside <choice>: the ink stays in <orig> or <abbr>, and the editor's reading goes in <reg resp="#editor"> or <expan resp="#editor">. The policy comes first and the tagging second. Consistency matters more than which standard a student picks.

Cohort Specific Normalization Guidance

JAY · Emily Dickinson Poem (MS Am 1118.3)

  • The poetic boundary: opake to opaque and etherial to ethereal can take <orig>/<reg>, which helps search.
  • Leave alone: capitals (Soul, Society, Door) and dashes are artistic choices. The policy should exclude them explicitly, so the ledger records the decision.

ACE · French Revolutionary Decree (1791)

  • The oi/ai shift: étoit to était is a clean orthographic rule. Set it up carefully, because it becomes Wednesday's best regex (and its best false-positive trap).
  • Accented capitals and long-s: DECRET/DÉCRET and ſ/s are typographical rules. Decide whether to mark them up or declare them in the header. art. and L.S. are abbreviations.

OLYVIA · Maud Lewis Letter (20th c. Cursive)

  • Vernacular is not error: allways, untill, truely, dont take <orig>/<reg>, never <sic>/<corr>.
  • Grammar stays: nonstandard agreement (we was) is voice, not spelling, and no rule touches it. Watch for class bias in what gets "fixed".

ANDROMEDA · 1752 Peace and Friendship Treaty

  • Thorn contractions: ye means the and yt means that; these are <abbr>/<expan>, not spelling variants. Govr and Esqr work the same way.
  • Capitalized substantives: lowercasing Articles and Peace is a defensible casing rule, but it must be one declared rule applied to every case. Leave Chief Cope's mark and the signatures untouched.
