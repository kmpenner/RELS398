Submission Instructions

  1. Build the repository in the shell. If you already have a course repository, use it and skip to step 2. Otherwise: 
    mkdir my-edition
cd my-edition
git init
echo "# [Short title of your document]" > README.md
git add README.md
git commit -m "Start edition repository with README title"
  2. Identify your witness in README.md. Under a heading ## Witness, record:
    • the holding institution and the shelfmark, call number, or reference number, exactly as the institution writes it;
    • what the object is: original manuscript, contemporary copy, later transcript, or printed item. If no original survives, say so and name the oldest extant exemplar;
    • the one page you are editing (recto/verso, sheet, or printed page) and what text it contains;
    • the URL of the image you are using, and whether it is a IIIF resource.
  3. Record the five checks. Under ## Checks, write one or two sentences for each, with the evidence you found:
    1. Image: is it legible at 100% zoom, and does it show the whole page with its edges?
    2. Identity: which catalogue or finding-aid record confirms the shelfmark? Give its link.
    3. Text: is there a published edition, transcription, or translation to compare against? If not, say so.
    4. Boundaries: does your page start and end at a describable point?
    5. Difficulty: in one sentence, what will be hard (hand, damage, language, layout)?
    A check that fails is still a valid result. Record it honestly, together with what you will do about it.
  4. Record rights and reuse. Under ## Rights, separate the rights in the object from the licence on the digital image. These often differ. Quote the licence or terms of use, and the credit line the institution asks for.
  5. Commit atomically. Commit after each of steps 2, 3, and 4, so your log shows at least three commits after the first. Each message should say what changed, specifically enough that someone reading only the log understands it (e.g. Record shelfmark and finding-aid link for witness, not update).
  6. Write your FAIR statement (200–300 words), in the submission box or under ## FAIR. For each of Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable, name one concrete decision in your project that serves it, and one way your particular document makes that principle harder.
  7. Submit: paste the full text of README.md, the output of git log --oneline, and your FAIR statement. If your repository is on GitHub, add the link as well.

Note. Your document is not a Greek manuscript, and some standard fields won't fit it. Where you make a judgement call, such as what counts as "one page" or whether an item is a copy, write one sentence saying why. Those sentences are what the reviewers grade most closely.
