PEDAGOGICAL CORE: THE GROUND TRUTH AS INVIOLABLE ANSWER KEY

Frame this hour with complete clarity: "Doing it by hand first is not an exercise in nostalgic labor. On Wednesday, Transkribus HTR will produce machine transcriptions; on Friday, an LLM will propose corrections. To measure their Character Error Rate (CER) and Word Error Rate (WER) in Week 5, you need an answer key. If you don't transcribe the page by hand today, you have no way to evaluate what the computer does tomorrow." Furthermore, transcription must be strictly diplomatic. Tidying up spelling or expanding abbreviations silently trains software to hallucinate modern conventions.

NOVICE TRAPS & BOUNDARY RULES: SILENT NORMALIZATION

Watch for students instinctively correcting irregular capitalization, archaic punctuation, or phonetic misspellings (especially in Maud Lewis's letter or 18th-century French). Emphasize that normalization is a Week 6 operation; today's loyalty is 100% to the physical ink on parchment or paper. Warn against putting text inside <gap/>: <gap/> is an empty tag for complete loss, whereas <unclear> holds tentative human readings.

Classroom Diagnostics & Troubleshooting Triage

  • Silent Normalization Trap: Student typed modern English/French spelling. Intervene immediately: "Type the scribe's error, not your correction."
  • Hyphen Insertion: When a word breaks across lines, do not insert modern hyphens unless an actual hyphen mark is present in the ink on the page.
  • Text Inside <gap/>: Schema validation error! <gap/> is an empty element; if there is a conjectured reading, use <unclear> or <supplied>.
  • Milestones Nesting: Ensure <lb/> and <pb/> are self-closing tags, not open wrappers around line contents.
  • Git Boundary Check: Ensure no temporary images or large TIFFs are staged; only witness.xml should be committed.
