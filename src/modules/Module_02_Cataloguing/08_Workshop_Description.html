Workshop: Codicological TEI Header & Authority Reconciliation

Overview: In this workshop you will catalogue your own chosen witness — one page of one document — and there are only four of you, so your upstream research becomes a shared class resource.

Each of you has selected a document that falls outside the Greco-Roman manuscript tradition that most cataloguing tools were built for. That is the point of the exercise, not an obstacle to it. You will identify which authority files actually govern your document, construct a validated TEI <msDesc> block describing it, and audit an LLM-generated metadata record for hallucinated codicological data.

The four witnesses:

  • Jay — a Dickinson manuscript (Amherst, 19th c.; American manuscript tradition). Note: her poems are open access while her letters are network-restricted, so the object has to be a poem or a poem enclosed in a letter.
  • Ace — a French declaration or decree of 1791 (Revolutionary France; post-1789 continental archival tradition, Archives nationales / Pierrefitte).
  • Olyvia — a handwritten letter by Maud Lewis (Nova Scotia folk artist, 1903–1970; 20th-century Canadian/Maritime personal correspondence).
  • Andromeda — the Peace and Friendship Treaty of 1752 (Mi'kma'ki / Nova Scotia; colonial legal instrument, printed bilingual proclamation, Nova Scotia Archives RG 1 vol 430 no 2).

A caution that applies to all four: a document is not a "manuscript witness" merely because it is old. Some of these objects are manuscript, some are print, and at least one has no surviving original at all. Deciding what your object is — and therefore which descriptive categories honestly apply to it — is the first and hardest part of this assignment.

Deliverables: a validated TEI <msDesc> snippet, plus a 300–500 word comparison of your manual catalogue against an LLM's attempt at the same task. Post your comparison for peer review.
