Before the Workshop: Three Things to Look At

These are short, and they are not background reading — each one answers a question you will hit in the workshop. Spend about twenty minutes in total. Come with the notes from step 3.

  1. Open a real catalogue record and read it slowly. Find your own document in its holding institution's online catalogue. Do not skim it. For every field you see, ask: is this describing the physical object, or the text written on it? Cataloguers separate those two things, and the TEI elements you will write — <msContents> for what it says, <physDesc> for what it is — are that same split. Write down two fields you could not confidently put in one box or the other.
  2. Look at a finished <msDesc> before you write one. In the TEI Guidelines, Chapter 10 (Manuscript Description) opens with complete worked examples. Read one all the way through without trying to understand every tag. You are looking for the shape: identifier first, then contents, then physical description. A <msDesc> is just a catalogue record written in XML — the same facts a library card holds, tagged so a computer can find each one.
  3. Try to look your document up in Trismegistos or VIAF, and expect to fail. Trismegistos indexes ancient texts; VIAF indexes names. None of your four documents is an ancient Greek manuscript, so at least one of these lookups will come back empty. That is the exercise, not a mistake. Note exactly what you searched and what came back, because "I checked this registry and it has no record of my document" is a finding you will be graded on — and it is the finding most students skip.

If a term stops you — shelfmark, witness, authority file, recto — it is defined in the lesson text for this module. Nothing here assumes you have met these words before this course.
