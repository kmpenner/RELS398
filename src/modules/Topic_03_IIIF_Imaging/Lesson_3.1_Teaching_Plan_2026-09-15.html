PEDAGOGICAL CORE: THE FACSIMILE AS PRIMARY HISTORICAL EVIDENCE

In digital scholarly editing, a facsimile is not a decorative illustration—it is primary material evidence that grounds every editorial claim. Today establishes the manual baseline across our cohort's four distinct physical document types. Before introducing algorithmic image processing (IIIF in 3.2) or machine vision (AI layout analysis in 3.3), students must understand how an image is physically created, calibrated with metric scale and color targets, and modeled in TEI coordinate space.

WORKSPACE DISCIPLINE & SCHEMA BOUNDARY

Remind students that <facsimile> is a sibling of <teiHeader> and <text> inside <TEI>, not a child of <text>. Placing it inside <text> will instantly trigger schema errors in Scholarly XML. Coordinates on ulx, uly, lrx, lry must be unitless integers representing the pixel grid.

Classroom Diagnostics & Troubleshooting Triage

  • Placement inside <text>: Schema validation error! <facsimile> must be an immediate child of <TEI>, situated between <teiHeader> and <text>.
  • Inverted coordinates: uly greater than lry. In TEI surface geometry, uly is the top edge (near 0) and lry is the bottom edge (near total height).
  • Broken @facs pointer: Ensure the attribute uses local fragment syntax (facs="#f1r_z1" with leading #) matching the target xml:id.
  • Unit syntax trap: Omit px on ulx, uly, lrx, lry (they must be raw integers); keep units only on @width / @height on <graphic>.
  • Scale bar calibration: Remind students that physical DPI/PPI calibration must be verified before declaring real-world metric dimensions in @unit.
