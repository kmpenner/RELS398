The Three Discrepancy Categories

Category A: Human Misread / Omission

Example: Human skipped a short word ("the", "and") or misread a minims sequence ("m" vs "in").

Action: Accept Gemini's reading into transcription_reconciled.xml. Note this in your assignment reflection: the AI served as a tireless proofreader catching human perceptual fatigue.

Category B: AI Hallucination / Smoothing

Example: Gemini modernized "ye" to "the", expanded "&c." to "et cetera", or invented missing letters over a smudge.

Action: Reject the machine reading; preserve your human manual transcription. Defend the physical ink: diplomatic transcription records what is on the page, not modern expectation.

Category C: Genuine Ambiguity (Ink Damage / Fade)

Example: Both human and machine struggled with a stained margin, cramped interlinear gloss, or faded ascender.

Action: Mark with <unclear>likelyWord</unclear> or <gap reason="damage" unit="chars" extent="3"/>. Scholarly honesty earns higher marks than guessing.

Category D: Physical vs. Logical Structure

Example: Line break (<lb/>) placement differed, or verse wraps were treated as separate stanzas.

Action: Enforce strict physical fidelity: <lb/> goes where the pen physically went to a new line, regardless of grammar or sentence structure.

  • Colloquial Orthography: If Gemini corrected Maud Lewis's vernacular spelling or grammar, reject the correction! The diplomatic text must preserve Maud's authentic voice.
  • Clefts and Folds: Check text written near folds or envelope margins; mark damaged words with <unclear>.
