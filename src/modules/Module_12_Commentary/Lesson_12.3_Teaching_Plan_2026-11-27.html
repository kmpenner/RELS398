Students authored manual commentary notes on Tuesday (establishing baseline ground truth) and tested rule-based text reuse algorithms on Wednesday. OPERATIONAL TARGET & CORE DIRECTIVE:
Each student prompts an AI model using structured negative constraints, conducts a forensic hallucination hunt on generated outputs, audits candidate notes against primary witnesses, records editorial decisions in COMMENTARY.md, and integrates vetted notes into TEI <standOff> with explicit @resp provenance.

  • Pedagogical connection: Evaluating commentary requires looking from both sides: celebrating computational power while maintaining sharp human skepticism against generative illusions.

AI writes with astonishing speed and fluency, but like looking at clouds from both sides, its smooth prose can hide illusions and fabricated facts. Today, you are the forensic editor who holds the model accountable.”

THE COMMENTARY DIRECTIVE: HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP FORENSIC AUDITING

A digital edition is not an uncritical echo of machine-generated text. Every AI draft must pass through a strict four-step forensic audit (quotations, entities, citations, and XML anchors) before being admitted into a scholarly edition.

INVIOLABLE PEDAGOGICAL BOUNDARIES

1. Zero trust for unvetted citations: Never accept a book title, volume number, or archival shelfmark without verifying it in primary library catalogs.
2. Negative prompt constraints mandatory: Every prompt must explicitly forbid invented citations and demand [UNVERIFIED] flags for uncertain claims.
3. Maintain transparent provenance: Always mark machine-generated notes with resp="#ai-draft" and human editors with resp="#human-editor".
4. The COMMENTARY.md ledger: Every student must maintain an audit ledger documenting decisions (Accept, Revise, Reject, Flag).
5. Assignment 12 due date: Assignment 12 (Scholarly Commentary, 80 points) is due Friday, December 4 at 11:00 PM.
