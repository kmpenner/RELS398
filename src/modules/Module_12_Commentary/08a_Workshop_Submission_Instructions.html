Submission Instructions

  1. Draft five scholarly commentary notes on your passage: at least one philological (explaining grammar/idiom), one historical/cultural (explaining realia/context), and one intertextual (documenting an allusion or scripture citation).
  2. Encode the notes in TEI XML using stand-off <back><div type="commentary"><note target="#token-or-anchor" type="...">...</note></div></back>.
  3. Link at least two notes to external Linked Open Data authorities (e.g. Pleiades URI for places, VIAF for historical figures, or CTS URN for classical passages).
  4. Run an intertextual text-reuse check (via Tesserae or targeted corpus search) and evaluate whether automated n-gram matching detected your allusion.
  5. Submit your TEI XML file and a 300–500 word reflection discussing the trade-offs between inline vs. stand-off commentary architecture.
