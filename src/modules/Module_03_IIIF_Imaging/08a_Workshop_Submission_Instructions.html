Submission Instructions

Work on the same one page you catalogued in Workshop 2. Everything below goes in your project repository and gets committed as you work.

  1. Get the best image you are allowed to use, and say where it came from. In most cases this is the holding institution's digitized surrogate. Record, in a file called IMAGE_PROVENANCE.md:
    • the URL you downloaded it from, and the date;
    • the file name, format, and file size;
    • whether a higher-resolution version exists that you did not take, and why (paywall, login, reading-room only, no larger version offered);
    • if you photographed or scanned the object yourself: the device, the lighting, whether a ruler or colour reference was in frame, and any handling constraint you worked under.
  2. Measure the image twice.
    • By eye: open it in a viewer and write down the width and height you see reported.
    • By tool: run file yourimage.jpg, or identify -format "%w x %h" yourimage.jpg if you have ImageMagick, or python3 labs/make_surface.py yourimage.jpg.
    • If your repository serves IIIF: fetch the image service's info.json and record the width and height it reports for the master. Note whether that master is larger than your download. If it does not serve IIIF, record that you checked and where you looked — a verified absence is a result, and it earns the same credit.
    Record all the numbers you got, including any that disagreed.
  3. Write the TEI <facsimile>. In your witness.xml, between </teiHeader> and <text>, add:
    • one <surface> with an @xml:id and the real @ulx @uly @lrx @lry of your image;
    • a <graphic> pointing at the image (a relative path if the file is in your repository, the full URL if it is not);
    • at least three <zone> elements marking features that matter on your document — a heading, a signature, a seal, a stamp, a column, a correction, a printer's line, an area of damage. Give each a @type you can defend;
    • at least one link from your text into the facsimile, using @facs on <pb>, <lb>, or <div>. The value starts with #.
    Validate against msdesc.rng and drive the Problems panel to 0 errors. Remember that the schema cannot check whether a @facs pointer aims at an id that exists — check that yourself.
  4. Settle the rights question in one short section of IMAGE_PROVENANCE.md. The rights in the object and the licence on the digital image are separate. State the licence or terms of use, quote the credit line the institution requires, and answer one question: are you permitted to produce a cropped or altered derivative of this image? If you are not, say what that rules out and note that zones point at regions without creating a new file.
  5. Choose one and do it:
    • If your document is served by IIIF: open its manifest, and report what it contains — how many Canvases, what the labels are, what metadata fields the institution chose to publish, and one thing you would want that is missing.
    • If it is not: write the case for what a manifest of your page would need to contain, and describe concretely what is currently lost by its absence — what can a reader of your edition not do that a reader of a IIIF-served edition can?
  6. Write a 300–500 word reflection on this question: where did your eye disagree with the tool, and which of you was wrong? Report your coordinate numbers, say what caused each discrepancy, and then address the part no tool settles — how you decided which three regions deserved a zone, and what a reader can check because you drew them.
  7. Submit: your <facsimile> block, your IMAGE_PROVENANCE.md, and the reflection. Commit your work as you go, not in one commit at the end.

Note on fit. The vocabulary of this module — folio, recto, opening, leaf — comes from bound manuscripts, and most of your documents are not bound manuscripts. Use the terms that honestly describe your object, and where you have to make a judgement call, write one sentence saying why. Those sentences are graded.
