THE ENTITY IDENTIFICATION OBJECTIVE: GROUND TRUTH BEFORE AUTOMATION

A computer cannot inherently know whether 'Paris' refers to the capital of France or a mythological Trojan prince, or whether 'Hopson' was a colonial governor or a private merchant. Automated entity recognition models make frequent boundary and type errors on historical documents. Therefore, students must establish an authoritatively verified manual ground truth before testing computational algorithms on Wednesday and Friday.

INVIOLABLE PEDAGOGICAL BOUNDARIES

1. Ground truth comes first: Today's 20-entity manual parse serves as the immutable gold-standard benchmark against which Wednesday's SpaCy NER model and Friday's LLM agent will be evaluated.
2. Standard authority registries: Link entities to canonical registries (Wikidata, VIAF, GeoNames, BnF, or DCB) using the @ref attribute. Never guess unverified URIs.
3. The Verified Absence rule: If exhaustive searching in reputable databases reveals no record for a historical actor, document a Verified Absence and mint a local project identifier (e.g. #person-001).
4. Temporal normalization: Every <date> tag must carry an ISO 8601 normalized date in the @when attribute (YYYY-MM-DD).
5. Assignment alignment: Work produced today builds the essential foundation for Assignment: Module 9 (Named-Entity Markup), due Friday, November 6, at 11:00 PM.
